## Abstract

**Background.** Reality monitoring — deciding whether an experience was perceived or imagined — is
often explained by sensory signal strength, by a higher-order evaluation of precision, or by an active
process that selects diagnostic tests and predicts their consequences. These accounts make overlapping
predictions on standard post-hoc tasks, so a passive confidence rating cannot adjudicate between them.

**Objective.** We test whether prospective prediction of a source-diagnostic test consequence adds
information about source attribution beyond signal strength and vividness, and whether a
prediction-confidence term adds explanatory value. The design also estimates whether dual counterfactual
prediction improves discrimination while increasing false-seen attribution for imagined items.

**Method.** Participants complete a preregistered source-monitoring task with a fixed sequence of
diagnostic tests. Confirmatory hierarchical Bayesian models `M0`, `M2`, `M4`, `M6`, and `M7` encode
signal-strength, prediction-consistency/dual-cost, vividness, and confidence/precision-proxy accounts.
The target design is evaluated with simulation-based calibration and model recovery before any human
data are collected. The primary outcome is a model-based prediction contrast; source accuracy and
false-seen rate are co-primary.

**Results.** Not available. No human participants have been run.

**Conclusions.** Not available. The study is designed to be able to reject the active-prediction
account, not to confirm it.

---

## Significance

Source-monitoring research has a measurement problem: the same confidence rating can be generated by
different processes. The contribution of this paper is not a new brain region or a clinical
instrument. It is a falsifiable behavioural-computational design that separates three accounts and
reports the result even when the active-prediction account loses.

If the design cannot separate the models, that is a publishable measurement result. If the human data
favour signal strength or precision, the paper reports that. If the data favour active prediction, the
paper still cannot claim decoding, clinical benefit, or a new natural kind.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The adjudication problem

Signal-strength accounts hold that reality judgments track the strength of the sensory evidence
[Dijkstra2023]. Higher-order precision accounts hold that judgments depend on an evaluation of the
reliability of that evidence [Dijkstra2024; DijkstraFleming2025; Fleming2026]. Active accounts hold
that people select tests, predict their consequences, and update source beliefs from prediction error.
These accounts are not mutually exclusive, and standard tasks often confound them.

### 1.2 What is new here

The design manipulates whether a prediction is diagnostic or non-diagnostic, whether the test sequence
is chosen actively or yoked, and whether vividness and confidence are manipulated orthogonally. The
confirmatory model set includes a strength-only model, prediction-consistency and dual-cost models, a
vividness model, and a confidence/precision-proxy model. The analysis is adversarial: a common evidence
metric, pre-specified priors, calibrated posteriors, and a decision rule that can return a null result.

This is not a priority claim. A systematic review is required before the manuscript uses "first" or
"novel" in a bibliographic sense.

### 1.3 Hypotheses

- **H1 (active prediction).** Prediction-consistency terms (`M2`/`M4`) improve source discrimination
  beyond signal strength and vividness.
- **H2 (dual cost).** The dual counterfactual term (`M4` vs `M2`) increases false-seen attribution for
  imagined items relative to single prediction.
- **H3 (precision proxy).** A prediction-confidence term (`M7`) explains variance that the strength-only
  model does not.
- **H0 (measurement).** The target design does not separate the confirmatory models; the study is
  underpowered or non-identifiable.

## 2. Model

Let `s ∈ {seen, imagined, described, novel}` be the latent source. The participant begins each block
with a uniform source belief `m_i(s)` and updates it from the observed outcome only. For a chosen test
`a`, the predicted outcome probability is

`q_i(a) = Σ_s m_i(s) p_as`.

The prediction report is a precision-weighted transform,

`P(P=1) = sigmoid(π_i logit(q_i(a)))`,

where `π_i` is a participant-level precision parameter. The source report is a categorical choice with
discriminability `d_i`, response bias `b_i`, vividness `γ_v`, confidence `γ_c`, and prediction-consistency
terms `δ_b` and `δ_dual`.

The confirmatory models differ in which source-report terms are free. All models include the
prediction-readout precision `α_π`; the table lists only the terms that distinguish the models.

| Model | Interpretation | Distinguishing source-report terms |
|---|---|---|
| `M0` | Signal strength only | `d`, `b` |
| `M2` | Prediction consistency | `d`, `b`, `δ_b` |
| `M4` | Prediction consistency plus dual cost | `d`, `b`, `δ_b`, `δ_dual` |
| `M6` | Vividness | `d`, `b`, `γ_v` |
| `M7` | Prediction confidence (precision proxy) | `d`, `b`, `γ_c` |

`M2` and `M4` are the active-prediction contrasts. `M6` is a vividness manipulation check, not an
active-prediction model. `M7` is a confidence/precision proxy, not a full hierarchical precision model.
The full implementation is in `analysis/crt/full_model.py`; the NumPyro mirror is in
`analysis/crt/numpyro_model.py`. The latent source is marginalized with the participant's belief; the
true source is not required for fitting.

## 3. Design and analysis plan

### 3.1 Design

- Target N=480 recruited, 432 analysed after pre-specified exclusions.
- 240 trials per participant, two occasions.
- Fixed test sequence with yoked active and passive arms.
- Vividness and confidence manipulated orthogonally in the calibration stage.
- Primary contrast: prediction consistency on diagnostic versus non-diagnostic tests.
- Co-primary: source accuracy and false-seen rate for imagined items.

### 3.2 Simulation gates

Before human data:

1. NUTS SBC per confirmatory model at the target design.
2. BIC recovery among the pre-specified models.
3. Design pairwise lower bound above the pre-specified threshold.
4. Calibration N=80, with a frozen likelihood table.

The target-scale package is `results/crt/CRT_STAGE0J_TARGET_SCALE.json`; the gate is
`ANIMUS_P5_CRT_STAGE0J_TARGET_SCALE`. It is currently `BLOCKED` because the publication-grade
posterior backend is unavailable.

### 3.3 Model comparison

- Pre-specified priors; no posterior tuning after seeing human data.
- SBC and recovery before fit.
- LOO/WAIC as secondary; no single fit index decides the theory.
- Equivalence region and a null-result decision rule.
- Multiverse of reasonable priors and exclusion rules reported in supplement.
- Independent statistician with masked analysis.

## 5. Discussion

The discussion will be written after the registered analysis. The possible outcomes are:

- **Active prediction wins.** Report the effect and the boundary; no decoding or clinical claim.
- **Confidence/precision proxy wins.** Report that the active-prediction operationalization is not
  incrementally valid, and that the proxy does not establish a full precision mechanism.
- **Strength wins.** Report that the task does not require an active process.
- **Models remain indistinguishable.** Report the negative identifiability result and narrow the
  measurement claim.

## 6. Limitations

- Simulation evidence is not human evidence.
- BIC recovery is not a NUTS marginal-likelihood comparison.
- The task may induce demand characteristics.
- The sample is likely WEIRD and online; generalization is limited.
- The model space is not exhaustive.
- The claim ceiling remains `L1_BEHAVIORAL_ASSISTED`.
